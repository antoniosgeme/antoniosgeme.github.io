<p class="research-lede">
My research sits at the intersection of <strong>unsteady fluid mechanics</strong>, <strong>flow sensing and control</strong>, and <strong>high-speed aerodynamics</strong>. I combine experiments, sensing, and reduced-order modeling to study how vehicles interact with unsteady and high-speed flows.
</p>

  <div class="thread-body">
    <h2>1. Unsteady aerodynamics &amp; vortex-dominated flows</h2>
    <p class="thread-tagline"><em>How do leading-edge vortices form, evolve, and shed when a wing meets a strong, unsteady disturbance?</em></p>
    <p>
      I study gust encounters and other strongly unsteady aerodynamic problems in which separation and vortex formation govern the force response. This work connects measured vorticity fields to transient lift and helps explain how three-dimensionality changes the evolution and breakdown of leading-edge vortices.
    </p>

  <div class="thread-body">
    <h2>2. Flow sensing, estimation &amp; closed-loop control</h2>
    <p class="thread-tagline"><em>What can a wing &ldquo;feel&rdquo; through its surface, and how do we use that signal to act?</em></p>
    <p>
      I use distributed surface sensing to estimate aerodynamic state in real time and to support control strategies for gust mitigation. The goal is to turn sparse on-body measurements into actionable information about lift, circulation, and flow state.
    </p>

    <h2>3. High-speed boundary-layer physics</h2>
    <p class="thread-tagline"><em>How do boundary layers transition and respond to disturbances at high speed — and can we sense those events on the body?</em></p>
    <p>
      My current postdoctoral work extends these experimental and sensing ideas into the high-speed regime. I focus on transition and boundary-layer dynamics on canonical geometries using IR thermography and surface instrumentation.
    </p>
    <h4>Current focus</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>IR-thermography measurements of transition onset and intermittency.</li>
      <li>Sensor-driven characterization of high-speed boundary layers.</li>
    </ul>
