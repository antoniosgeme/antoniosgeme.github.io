<p class="teaching-lede">
I think computation is one of the most effective ways to learn fluid mechanics and aerodynamics. Good computational demos let students move beyond memorizing equations: they can vary parameters, test intuition, and see how mathematical structure translates into physical behavior. My goal in teaching is to use computation not as a substitute for theory, but as a way to make theory more concrete, interactive, and memorable.
</p>

## Teaching Philosophy

I want students to build intuition by moving back and forth between mathematics, physical interpretation, and computation. In practice, that means using computational tools to let students explore what equations mean, why approximations work, and where they break down. The strongest learning happens when a student can derive an idea on paper, test it computationally, and then explain the result in physical terms.

I am especially interested in teaching approaches where simple interactive models help students develop a feel for difficult concepts before they encounter more realistic or higher-fidelity methods. In aerodynamics, that includes using low-order models, visualization, and interactive parameter studies to build understanding of circulation, conformal mapping, unsteady lift, and flow control.
